## Findings

### 1. A new holding company is never a deal: `DEAL`'s "become … holding compan" branch cannot match, so Goldman Sachs's and BMO's conversions make no row · `P2`

**How sure:** high. These are the repository's regex, `isDeal` and `h2Roles` over the committed file.

**Evidence**
- **The regex.** `DEAL` (`lib/fedH2.ts:493–494`) closes every alternative with `)\b`. Its branch `become (?:a |the )?(?:bank|financial|savings and loan|thrift) holding compan` is always followed by "y" or "ies", so the closing `\b` never holds. On "to become a bank holding company." the branch alone returns `false`.
- **161 actions are refused for this reason alone.** They pass every other test `isDeal` makes (4 of them the Board's).
- **20 of them name a listed company** by `h2Roles`. From the fixtures' first prices, five are on pages:
  - **GS 2008-09-21, the Board:** "The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc., New York, New York, and Goldman Sachs Bank USA Holdings LLC — to become bank holding companies." Approved. Morgan Stanley's action that day reaches MS only because its text adds "engage".
  - **BMO 2000-04-10, the Board:** "Bank of Montreal … — to become financial holding companies."
  - **PNBK 1999-09-10 and FBSI 2014-01-03:** "to become a bank holding company …".
  - **UBS 2026-02-05:** the Board denied a petition on UBS's "proposal to become bank holding companies".
  - The other 15 have no first price in the fixtures: NWBI 2020-11-04, HTB (3), SFBC (2), FNWB (2), FSBW, FVCB, BSRR, PKBK, ENBP, GSBC 1998, SFDL.
- **The docs say otherwise.** `lib/fedH2.ts:30–31` and `:492`, and `CLAUDE.md:236`, list "a new holding company" among the deals.
- **The check never isolates the branch.** Every "become" sentence check:fed-h2 asserts also holds another deal word: "… and to acquire Signature Bank", "election to become …", "(1) to become … by merging" (`scripts/check-fed-h2.ts:145–148`).

**What users see:** GS's page has no row, and the Board's release of that day no H.2 line, for its conversion into a bank holding company in September 2008. Nineteen other listed banks lose an action the same way.

**What a fix would need:** end that branch `compan(?:y|ies)` or drop its boundary, and add a check sentence with no other deal word.

*Importance:* high: landmark events missing from bank pages · *Efficiency:* one regex · *Practicality:* trivial.

### 2. SLFPY, the old Standard Life's record, gets the 2026 deal of the company now called Standard Life plc · `P2`

**How sure:** high that the party is another company, by the committed file's own notices. The row shows only if SLFPY has prices on 2026-11-09; the fixtures do not say.

**Evidence**
- **The record.** `data/edgar-tickers.json` holds `[1370418, "SLFPY", "STANDARD LIFE PLC"]`. Its own rows show it is the Standard Life that merged with Aberdeen in 2017:
  - its HSR row of 2017-05-18 is "Standard Life plc's acquisition involving Aberdeen Asset Management PLC";
  - its H.2 row of 2017-08-11 is MUFG "to acquire shares of Standard Life, plc, Edinburgh".
- **The same group's later names.** The EU file calls it "Standard Life Aberdeen" (M.8817, 2018) and "Aberdeen Group plc" (M.12329, received 2026-01-27; M.12414, received 2026-04-30).
- **The 2026 notice.** M.12645, received 2026-11-09, names "Standard Life plc ('Standard Life', United Kingdom)". It calls Standard Life Assurance Limited "currently a wholly-owned subsidiary of Standard Life". That is a different company from the Aberdeen Group of the same year.
- **The row.** `euEvents` for CIK 1370418 gives "European Commission received notification of Standard Life plc, CVC Capital Partners plc and 1 others' acquisition of Standard Life Assurance Limited (M.12645)", with "the company is an acquirer (Standard Life plc)".
- **The cause.** `NOT_THE_COMPANY` (`lib/euMergers.ts:383–398`) has no entry for 1370418. No rule reads a name another company took up after the record's own company dropped it; `TAKEN_NAMES` covers only the reverse.

**What a fix would need:** refuse "Standard Life plc" on 1370418 from the day its group stopped being Standard Life plc, with that day read from a filing.

*Importance:* medium: another company's deal on a page · *Efficiency:* one entry · *Practicality:* easy.

### 3. A Board release two companies share takes the H.2 line of whichever company ingested last; Morgan Stanley's 2008-09-21 action then shows nowhere on its page · `P3`

**How sure:** high. Reproduced on PostgreSQL 16 with `upsertEvent` and `boardReleaseDays`.

**Evidence**
- **One event for every page.** A `fed_press` row has `dedupBasis: fed-press:<release path>` (`lib/fedPress.ts:250`).
- **One H.2 line per company.** The line is built from that company's own Board actions (`lib/ingest/fedPressFor.ts:26–27`, `lib/fedPress.ts:237`).
- **The body is rewritten** whenever the content hash changes (`lib/ingest/store.ts:1325–1335`).
- **`fed_h2` drops a Board action** on any day the page holds a Board release (`lib/fedH2.ts:776`).
- **The reproduction.** Release `bcreg20080921a.htm` ("Board approves … the applications of Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley") was stored by MS's answer, then GS's, twice over:
  - after each MS store the body carries "… lists it: … Morgan Stanley … — to become bank holding companies and engage in certain nonbanking activities. Approved, September 21, 2008.";
  - after each GS store the line is gone;
  - `boardReleaseDays(MS)` holds the day, so MS's `fed_h2` gives 0 rows for it.
- **Scale.** Offline, 17 releases are on two or more pages and this one diverges. With finding 1 fixed, GS would write its own, different line, and the row would keep flipping.

**What a fix would need:** build the line from every Board action of the day naming any of the release's parties, so every page writes the same text.

*Importance:* low to medium · *Efficiency:* one join · *Practicality:* moderate.

### 4. `sync:eu-mergers` rebuilds both notice lists from each run's answers: one year can come back empty and pass the floor, and ingest then unlinks its rows · `P3`

**How sure:** high for the mechanism. It needs one faulty endpoint answer.

**Evidence**
- **The lists come only from this run.** `notifications` and `nonopposition` are written from the year queries alone (`scripts/sync-eu-mergers.ts:145–146`); only the texts are carried over (`:124`).
- **A 404 becomes an empty year.** `get` returns null on a 404 (`:54`), a rule written for CELLAR, and `year()` reads null as `{}` for the SPARQL endpoint too (`:74`).
- **The only guard is a total floor** of 10,000 and 8,000 (`:123`). The file holds 10,264 notifications and 8,507 non-oppositions.
  - Any one of 1990–1998, 2003, 2004 or 2009 can vanish without tripping it (11 to 256 notifications, 10 to 65 read texts each).
  - So can any single non-opposition year: none holds more than 411.
- **What follows.** `euMergers()` builds notifications only from the list, so a lost year's texts are orphaned. `euMergersFor` returns `complete: {}`, and `runSource` calls `unlinkGone` (`scripts/ingest.ts:303`).
- **The sibling does better.** `sync:hsr` keeps everything the committed file holds (`scripts/sync-hsr.ts:55`).

**What a fix would need:** keep every notice once listed, or check each year against the held count.

*Importance:* low to medium: silent loss of rows · *Efficiency:* small · *Practicality:* easy.

### 5. `sync:fed-h2` holds for ever a week whose layout `readH2` does not know · `P3`

**How sure:** high.

**Evidence**
- **How a week is read.** `readH2` calls a text a scan only under 2,000 characters (`lib/fedH2.ts:253`), and reads only inside a section heading it knows (`:265–282`).
- **The test.** A 6,152-character release of 40 well-formed actions:
  - under the heading "Federal Reserve Actions on Applications" gives 0 actions, `scanned: false`, `unsplit: 0`;
  - under "Board Actions" it gives 40.
- **The sync then keeps the week.** It holds it (`scripts/sync-fed-h2.ts:64`), never asks for it again (`:56`), and counts nothing for it.
- **None today.** No held week in the committed file lacks actions without being listed unread.

**What a fix would need:** leave unheld, or fail, a week of release length that gives no action, and record per-week counts as `sync:hsr` does.

*Importance:* low · *Efficiency:* a few lines · *Practicality:* easy.

### 6. EU notices read short: 12 case numbers written without "Case" are lost with their clearance lines, and a three-party list loses its first party · `P3`

**How sure:** high.

**Evidence**
- **The word "Case" is required.** `caseOf` and `titleParties` need it (`lib/euMergers.ts:87`, `:93`). So 12 titles such as "(M.6968 — Lufthansa/CAE/JV)" and "(M.8440 — DuPont/FMC (Health and Nutrition business))" give no case.
- **Lost clearance lines.** For 11 of the 12 a non-opposition notice of that number follows, but `clearanceOf` returns null without a case (`:375`).
- **Rows affected.** The replay finds 7 rows on these notices, 5 from the fixtures' first prices. DD's and FMC's rows of 2017-06-07 are titled "European Commission received notification of a concentration".
- **A lost first party.** M.2096's "the undertakings Bayer AG, Deutsche Telekom AG and Infraserv GmbH & Co. Höchst KG (Infraserv) acquire" is read as the one party "Deutsche Telekom AG and Infraserv GmbH & Co. Höchst KG". `nameBefore` stops at the comma, so Bayer AG is lost. Bayer is not in the index today.

**What a fix would need:** read `M.\d+` without "Case", and split "A, B and C".

*Importance:* low · *Efficiency:* small · *Practicality:* easy.
