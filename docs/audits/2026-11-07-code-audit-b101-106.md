## Findings

### 1. A row whose list of cases grows says "· 2 sources", or up to 5, when one agency attests it; batch 106 brings it to SEC and CFPB day rows · `P2`

**How sure:** high. Reproduced on a real Postgres through `upsertEvent` and `loadEvents`; the counts come from replays of the real data.

**Evidence**

*The mechanism.*
- `loadEvents` counts every attestation of an event (`lib/store/read.ts:248`). When there is more than one, it ends the row's description with "· N sources" (`:275-276`), under the comment "surface corroboration where it exists — 'in 12 papers' is a real signal".
- An attestation is keyed by event, source and external id (`lib/ingest/store.ts:1069-1079`, `db/001_init.sql:113`). A new external id under the same dedup key is a new attestation, and the old one stays.
- Batch 106 makes an SEC litigation or CFPB day row carry every case's number (`lib/secCases.ts:281`, `lib/cfpb.ts:185`). The ledger states the consequence: "Ingest keeps the old number's attestation and adds the new one, so the set only grows" (`MASTER-CHECKLIST.md:1019`).
- EPA (`lib/epaCases.ts:435`, `:459`), the CPSC (`lib/cpscRecalls.ts:282`, `:291`), the CFTC (`lib/cftc.ts:161`) and the Digest's month rows (`lib/secDigest.ts:973`) already joined their numbers.

*On a real database* (`audit107/probes/db_reingest.ts`, output in `audit107/out/db_reingest.txt`). Each row was stored first as batches 83–105 stored it, then again as batch 106 builds it, under the same dedup key:

| Page | Row | First ingest | After the re-ingest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regions (RF) | 2009-09-21 "SEC litigation release: Regions Bank" | "… (file 3-13618): Regions Bank" | "… (file 3-13618): Regions Bank · 2 sources" |
| JPMorgan | 2015-01-22 CFPB, Genuine Title | "…kickback scheme they..." | "…kickback scheme they... · 2 sources" |
| Wells Fargo | 2015-01-22 CFPB, Genuine Title | "…kickback scheme they..." | "…kickback scheme they... · 2 sources" |

The re-ingest made no new event (`newEvents` 0) and three new attestations, one a row.

*How many rows.*
- **SEC and CFPB:** batch 106's own list of the saved data (`b106/multi.txt`) holds 38 SEC company-days and 9 CFPB company-days of two or more cases. Among them are Baxter's three proceedings of 2022-02-22, American Express's three actions of 2012-10-01 and of 2013-12-24, and Bank of America's two of 2023-07-11. Each one stored before batch 106 gains a second attestation at its next ingest. Any day row that learns a later case of the same day does the same.
- **EPA:** batch 104's units added a case to 17 stored rows under the same day, on 8 companies: ExxonMobil 10; Chevron, Home Depot, Lockheed Martin, Nucor, Kimberly-Clark, Bunge and Clean Harbors 1 each (`b104/per-old.json` against `per-new.json`). That is a floor: rows whose day became a month are not counted.
- **CPSC:** weekly reads of the real answer from 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-05, the company ingested after each (`audit107/probes/cpsc_attest.ts`):

  | Company | Rows stored | Rows that would read "· N sources" | Highest N |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Amazon | 22 | 22 | 5 |
  | Walmart | 29 | 22 | 5 |
  | Target | 21 | 13 | 3 |
  | Home Depot | 17 | 10 | 3 |

  Amazon's January 2025 row would read "CPSC: 14 recalls of products sold in its stores, January 2025 · 4 sources". The 4 is neither the recalls nor the agencies.
- **Digest:** a month's 8-K row grows while the walk is part-way through, 60 issues a run.

*Why the checks pass.* check:also-on, check:sec-cases and check:cfpb build the events once and never store them twice. No check reads `loadEvents`' description after a re-ingest.

**Failure scenario:** JPMorgan's page shows the CFPB's Genuine Title action as "· 2 sources": one agency, one action. Amazon's recent months read as if several sources confirmed each one.

**Smallest fix:** count distinct sources, not attestations: `count(DISTINCT x.source_id)` at `read.ts:248`. "Also on" keeps working, since it reads the attestations themselves.

**Check:** re-ingest one SEC day row with a grown key, against a stand-in database, and assert the description has no "sources" for one source.

*Importance:* medium-high: a trust signal, false on the busiest pages · *Efficiency:* one word of SQL · *Practicality:* trivial.

### 3. One year page of the Digest's archive that reads as empty, or fails once, stops every walk before any issue is read · `P3`

- **(b)** A hand list for the two pairs, per source, by place.
