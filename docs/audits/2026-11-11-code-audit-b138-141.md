## Summary

The SQL of all three batches works against a real Postgres. The FDA list (3,807 letters), MSHA's findings (1,102 investigations, 1,966 violations) and EPA's whole case file (137,103 cases) all went in and came back with no differences. Six defects, one `P2` and five `P3`:
- **EPA projects on the wrong day (P2).** Batch 141 adds the projects of a case's settlements onto one row even where the case keeps one row on another day. So four rows carry $23,829,634 of projects from settlements of other years. BP's 2015 Deepwater Horizon row shows MOEX's projects of 2012, the very case the code's own comment warns of.
- **FDA close-out day (P3).** The "close-out letter" day on an FDA row is the day the FDA posted it, not the letter's day. This affects all 18 such letters on pages.
- **Four FDA letters missed (P3).** They went to Reckitt, ADM, Orthofix and Novo Nordisk units the hand list would take.
- **Amazon in EPA (P3).** The Amazon lead is real, but in EPA rather than through the ".com" caption gap. EPA has no Amazon unit, so Amazon Services LLC's $1,215,700 pesticide order of 2018 is on no page. The iRhythm lead is not a defect elsewhere: ingest reads "iRhythm Technologies, Inc." from EDGAR's former names.
- **Control characters (P3).** EPA's project texts keep Windows-1252 quotes as invisible control characters on three page rows.
- **Docs and comments (P3).** A few contradict the data, including the hand list's reason for iRhythm.

Fix 1 first. It is one rule in `projectsOf`.

## Findings

### 1. EPA projects from a settlement of another day, on BP's page another company's, sit on four rows · `P2`

**How sure:** high. This was computed on EPA's whole file with the repository's own `readCaseFile` and `epaEvents`.

**Evidence**
- **The rule.** `projectsOf` adds every settlement's `SEP_AMT` (`lib/epaCases.ts:425-429`). The penalty, the work ordered and the cost recovery on the same row take EPA's case total, else the one settlement's figure (`:453-456`). The comment just above says why: "a case of several keeps its own total, since a sum would put other parties' settlements of other years on one row (Deepwater Horizon's four, 2012–2015)" (`:416-418`).
- **When a case keeps one row.** `bySettlement` gives each settlement day its own row only for a court case. `epaEvents` keeps those rows only on a page whose own defendants are all of the case's defendants that day (`:1465`). "A case of several parties keeps one row" (`:1403`), on the case's own day, and that row gets the sum.
- **What the whole file gives** (`probes/epa_mixed.ts`, `out/epa_mixed.txt`; `probes/epa_bp.ts`, `out/epa_bp.txt`). Four rows from first prices show projects from a settlement of another day. On each, that settlement's penalty is not shown, because the case has no EPA total and several settlements:
  - **BP, 2015-11-30, Deepwater Horizon** (cases 04-2010-9036 and 06-2011-4881, 10 defendants each). The row reads "supplemental environmental projects $10,000,000, EPA's figure, among 10 parties", once for each case. Both $10,000,000 come from the settlements of 2012-02-17, named "BP EXPLORATION aka MOEX (GULF OF MEXICO OIL SPILL)" in EPA's settlements table: MOEX Offshore's. The settlements of 2015-11-30 have none.
  - **BP, 2021-12-02, Whiting refinery** (case 05-2007-3933, 2 defendants): $2,000,000 from the settlement of 2012-05-23, whose $8,000,000 penalty is not on the row.
  - **ConocoPhillips, 2012-02-28** (case 06-2000-1237): $1,600,000 from the settlement of 2005-01-27, whose $1,719,498 penalty is not on the row.
  - **Owens Corning, 2018-01-30** (administrative case 05-2017-5026): $1,229,634, of which $229,634 came with the order of 2017-03-27, whose $90,000 federal penalty is not on the row.
- **How much.** $23,829,634 of the $181,844,220.07 the batch counts from first prices sits on a day other than its settlement's, by up to nine years.
- **Why the count of 27 missed it.** The 27 settlements in multi-settlement cases "not split by day" (21 cases, which reproduce: `out/epa_pg.txt`) are the cases `bySettlement` does not split. The BP and ConocoPhillips cases are court cases it does split, and `epaEvents` then keeps them as one row. Of the 21, only Owens Corning's reaches a page with figures from two days. In 16 of them, only one settlement has a figure (`out/epa_unsplit.json`).

**What users see:** BP's page says its Deepwater Horizon settlements of 2015-11-30 came with $20,000,000 of projects "among 10 parties". In EPA's file they belong to MOEX's settlements of 2012.

**The question asked, answered:** summing is right where the sum stays on its settlement's day: a case split by day, or settlements all on one day. It is wrong where a case of several settlements keeps one row on another day. There the cost-recovery rule (EPA's total, else the one settlement's) would show nothing, as the penalty beside it already does.

**What a fix would need:**
- On a row that stands for a case of several settlements, give only the projects of the settlements dated that row's day. Or follow the cost-recovery rule.
- Pin the four rows in check:epa-cases.

*Importance:* medium: a $20,000,000 figure on the wrong day, from another company's settlement, on a large company's page · *Efficiency:* one rule and four pins · *Practicality:* easy.

**What users see:** quotes and apostrophes that show as nothing or as an empty box, in EPA's words on three rows.

6. **`fetchLetters`' completeness rule holds on today's list** (0 duplicate paths). Two cases would refuse a week's read, and the kept letters stay either way:
   - a list holding one letter on two rows would be refused every week;
   - when the list's total is a whole thousand, both directions' page edges fall on the same rows.
7. **`projectsQuoted`'s cut never splits a word.** The longest unbroken run in any EPA project text is 35 characters.
