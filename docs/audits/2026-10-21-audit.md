## Summary

The mechanics are clean: tsc, lint, build and all 35 offline suites are green, and the new macro parsers match the Fed's and BLS's real record year by year. The most serious defect comes from batch 22. The price adapter rounds every close to whole cents. For decades of split-adjusted history that rounding invents moves: Walmart's "Biggest moves" panel shows six +100% days that never happened, and `/compare` puts NVIDIA's return 13,000 points too high. Because a full history is read only once, the damage is permanent for every company ingested until it is fixed, so fix it before the next ingest run.

## Findings

### 1. Every close is rounded to cents, so decades of split-adjusted history store fake moves, and the full history is read only once · `P0`

**Evidence:**
- **Where the rounding happens.** `lib/prices.ts:119` stores `value: Math.round(c * 100) / 100`. The column it goes into is `close numeric(18,6)` (`db/001_init.sql:206`).
- **The panel.** `components/BiggestMoves.tsx:93-105` computes "largest single-day price changes" over the whole stored series (`CompanyExplorer.tsx:258` passes in every row from `loadPrices`).
- **Live run.** I ran the panel's own algorithm on the app's `fetchDailyPrices(t, "full")` output, then on Yahoo's unrounded closes:

| Ticker | Panel shows (stored, cent-rounded) | Yahoo's real closes |
|---|---|---|
| WMT | **six "+100.0%" days**, 1973-04-24 … 1975-06-17 (0.01 → 0.02) | largest move −16.8%; 1973-07-24 was +12.3% |
| NVDA | 1999-04-27, 06-03, 06-21 each **+33.3%** (0.03 → 0.04) | 1999-06-03 was 0.034375 → 0.035286, **+2.6%**. Real moves pushed off the panel: −35.2% (2004-08-06), −30.7% (2008-07-03) |
| AAPL | 1997-08-06 +27.8% | +33.2% |
| HD | 1981-11-02 +25.0% | +25.7% |

- **`/compare`.** `components/PriceOverlay.tsx:166-168` shows the returns. On the app's own `growthOf100`, AAPL vs NVDA from 1999-01-22 gives NVDA **+541,075.0%**; the unrounded closes give **+527,669.6%**. AAPL comes out +96,900% against +98,026%.
- **Scale.** Below $1: 5,954 of AAPL's 11,556 closes, 4,355 of NVDA's 6,979 and 3,595 of WMT's. **MNST stores 110 closes as `$0.00`.**

**What is wrong:** Until batch 22, rounding to cents cost little on ten years of mostly double-digit closes. Split-adjusted closes from the 1970s–1990s are fractions of a cent to a few cents, and at that scale a one-cent tick is 25–100%.

**Why it matters:** The panel and the compare figure are the numbers on these pages a reader is most likely to quote. They are now wrong on Dow components, and the panel ranks the artefacts above real crashes.

The damage cannot heal by itself:
- `hasFullPriceHistory` (`lib/ingest/store.ts:661-675`) makes the full read happen once per subject ("read once per subject, not once per run").
- After that, refreshes only upsert the last ten years (`scripts/ingest.ts:577-579`).
- So every pre-2016 close stored by today's code stays rounded until a split happens or someone deletes the log row by hand.

**Nothing checks this:**
- No check covers price precision or the panel's arithmetic.
- `check:live` asserts "no zero close" (`scripts/check-live.ts:134`) only on AAPL (`:261`), where the smallest close rounds to $0.05.

**Suggested fix:** Store the precision the column already allows, rounding for display only. Then invalidate the `… full history` log rows written before the fix so each subject's history is re-read once.
*Importance:* high: wrong numbers, persisted · *Efficiency:* very high: one line plus a one-time re-read · *Practicality:* very high.

### 2. The committed-index name match trips on punctuation and sends household names to the wrong security · `P1`

**Evidence:** `lib/sec.ts:166-170` (`prefixHit`) matches with `title.toUpperCase().startsWith(q + " ")` on the raw EDGAR title. A comma, hyphen or apostrophe therefore decides the answer:
- BLK's title is `BlackRock, Inc.`, which does not start with `BLACKROCK `.
- KO's is `COCA COLA CO`, while CCEP's is `COCA-COLA EUROPACIFIC PARTNERS plc`.

`resolveSearch` with the database and network refused (the path every not-yet-held security takes):

```
"Coca-Cola"                      -> company CCEP
"how did covid affect Coca-Cola" -> company CCEP focus=covid
"BlackRock"                      -> company BSTZ   (BlackRock Science & Technology Term Trust)
"Google"                         -> company DEPW   (fund-name rung: "Google DeepMind AI Lab Ecosystem ETF")
"Home Depot"                     -> company HD focus=Depot      (salvaged "Home", tail kept as a focus)
"Home Depot earnings"            -> company HD focus=Depot earnings
"Lowe's"                         -> suggest: three Fidelity "U.S. Low Volatility" funds
"Lowe's stock"                   -> company LOW
"McDonald's"                     -> suggest only;  "McDonalds" -> MCD
```

Across the whole index, 2,410 companies have a title of the form `Name, Inc.` / `Name, Corp.`. For **2,347** of them the name before the comma resolves locally to nothing, so search falls back to "did you mean". For **28** it resolves to a different security: `BlackRock`→BSTZ, `Cheniere Energy`→CQP, `Banco Santander`→BSBR, `POPULAR`→MMYXX. The same function backs `/company/<name>` (`lib/page-data.ts:281`), so `/company/BlackRock` gathers and shows BSTZ.

**What is wrong:** The scored, punctuation-tolerant matching built for held subjects (`findCandidates`, closed 2026-08-12) was never applied to the committed index, which answers every security not yet held. Separately, batch 19's possessive handling (`lib/resolveSearch.ts:120-127`) only runs when the parser changed the query, so a bare "Lowe's" or "McDonald's" never reaches it.

**Why it matters:** Search is the site's front door. A confident page for the wrong company is worse than a miss, because the chart under the reader's question belongs to someone else. "Home Depot" also narrows a correct page to an unrequested "Depot" filter that hides dividends and macro rows.

**Suggested fix:** Compare normalised names (punctuation and legal suffixes folded, the way `commonName` and `lib/fuzzy.ts` already do) before the prefix test, and give the possessive variants to the whole-query attempt too.
*Importance:* high · *Efficiency:* high, all local · *Practicality:* high. The "3M" fix shows the rung is easy to change safely under `check:tickers`.

### 3. "Before the ticker" calls the start of Yahoo's dataset a company's first trading day · `P2`

**Evidence:**
- `components/CompanyExplorer.tsx:157` sets `firstTrade = prices[0]?.time`.
- `:243-246` renders "*{n} events before the first trading day ({firstTrade}) — the run-up to going public*".
- Live `fetchDailyPrices(t, "full")` first closes: **IBM, GE, KO, BA and DIS all 1962-01-02**. Ford 1972-06-01. JPM 1980-03-17.

**What is wrong:** 1962-01-02 is where Yahoo's daily data begins, not when any of these listed:
- GE was in the original 1896 Dow.
- Ford's 1956 IPO would be filed under "run-up to going public", with Ford's "first trading day" printed as 1972-06-01.

The new prose repeats the claim as "every daily close since listing":
- `docs/COVERAGE-MAP.md:34` ("IBM from 1962");
- `README.md:513`;
- `scripts/ingest.ts:572`;
- `lib/ingest/store.ts:664`;
- NC-077's closure.

Before batch 22 the heading was wrong for every company listed before 2016. It is now wrong for those listed before Yahoo's data starts.

**Why it matters:** A date on screen is a claim the reader can check, and this one is false on the pages of the oldest, most-searched companies.

**Suggested fix:** Label it "before our earliest price ({date})" unless a real listing date is known, and drop "since listing" from the docs.
*Importance:* moderate · *Efficiency:* very high, copy only · *Practicality:* very high.

### 4. Large filers' page data is 2–3× over Next's 2 MB cache limit, so the 15-minute page cache never applies to them · `P2`

**Evidence:**
- `lib/page-data.ts:185` caches held company pages with `unstable_cache`.
- `node_modules/next/dist/server/lib/incremental-cache/index.js:530-541` refuses a fetch-cache item whose `JSON.stringify` is over 2 MB. It warns in production and throws in dev.
- `loadEvents` has no `LIMIT` (`lib/store/read.ts:236-250`).

I shaped live `getFilingsDeep(…, true)` output, the full price history and today's 470 macro rows the way the loader returns them. Filings, prices and macro only, before any news or Wikipedia rows:

|---|---|---|---|

Batch 22 records "*The cached page stays well under the 2 MB cache limit*" (`docs/MASTER-CHECKLIST.md:1006-1007`). That was measured on the demo seed, where Ford holds 49 events.

**What is wrong:** For the largest filers NC-159's fix silently switches off: every view re-runs the full query set. ### 5. The rate limiter keeps visitors' IP addresses, and the Federal Register terms they typed, until 10,000 clients pile up; the privacy draft says "within an hour" · `P2`

**Evidence:**
- `app/legal/privacy/page.tsx:44`: the per-IP counts "*are never written down and disappear within an hour*".
- `lib/rateLimit.ts:37` and `:82` remove expired windows only when the map already holds `maxKeys = 10_000` clients.

Run against the real classes:

```
FixedWindowLimiter keys held 30 days later: 2 [ '203.0.113.7', '198.51.100.9' ]
DistinctLimiter keys held 30 days later: [ '203.0.113.7', '198.51.100.9' ]
  terms kept for the first IP: [ 'export controls' ]
```

**What is wrong:** On a low-traffic site an IP address stays in process memory until restart. `DistinctLimiter` keeps the search terms that IP sent alongside it. `check:legal`, which exists "to keep the privacy page honest", does not test this sentence.

**Why it matters:** The privacy page is a draft (NC-124 open), but it is linked from every footer. It was written "from what the code does", and on this point the code does something else. It is the kind of statement a lawyer's read will rely on.

**Suggested fix:** Sweep expired windows on access or on a timer, or reword the page. Add a `check:legal` case that ties the sentence to the behaviour.
*Importance:* moderate (legal accuracy) · *Efficiency:* high · *Practicality:* high.
