## Findings

### 1. The merger notices' comma splitter breaks a name with a comma into parties: NSC's page says Norfolk Southern was acquired, BDX loses its own four deals, and check:hsr pins the split as correct · `P2`

**How sure:** high. These are the repository's `readGrantNotice` and `hsrEvents` over the committed file and the committed fixture.

**Evidence**
- **The splitter.** `byCommas` splits at every ", " that is not followed by a legal word (`lib/hsrEarly.ts:148–155`). `splitParties` uses it whenever a cell has fewer than three semicolon chunks (`:144–145`).
  - So "Clayton, Dubilier & Rice Fund V Limited Partnership" becomes two parties. So do "Warburg, Pincus …", "Welsh, Carson, Anderson & Stowe …", "Hicks, Muse, Tate & Furst …" and "Becton, Dickinson and Company".
- **146 grants are affected.** Their acquiring person is the first word of such a name: Hicks 35, Golder 25, Warburg 24 (+1 "Warbug"), Welsh 19, Code 18, Bruckmann 6, Sears 5, Becton 4, Kidd 3, Clayton 3, Dimeling 2, Baker 1.
  - In each, the second word becomes the acquired person and the real acquired person becomes an entity. For example, `['98-1460','1998-01-30','Clayton','Dubilier & Rice Fund V Limited Partnership',['Norfolk Southern Corporation','North American Van Lines, Inc.'],…,'98-3505']`.
- **Rows `hsrEvents` builds, from the fixtures' first prices:**
  - **NSC 1998-01-30:** "…ended the merger waiting period early on Clayton's acquisition of Norfolk Southern Corporation and North American Van Lines, Inc. from Dubilier & Rice Fund V Limited Partnership", with "(the company as an acquired entity)".
  - **PEP 1997-07-18:** "Bruckmann's acquisition of Sherrill & Co., L.P. and 2 other entities from Rosser".
  - **THC 1997-12-05:** "Welsh's acquisition of Anderson & Stowe VII, L.P. and 2 other entities from Carson".
  - **ITT 1997-08-28:** "Clayton's acquisition of ITT Corporation and ITT Information Services, Inc. from Dubilier & Rice Fund V Limited Partnership".
- **BDX's own deals are lost.** 97-1602 (Difco), 97-1817 (PharMingen), 98-1015 (MedPlus) and 20110575 (Accuri) all read as acquiring "Becton", acquired "Dickinson and Company". `hsrEvents` for CIK 10795 gives no row on any of the four days.
- **It is not only 1994–1998.** The committed fixture's notice 2011-8331 prints `20110575 G Becton, Dickinson and Company; Accuri Cytometers, Inc.; ⏎Becton, Dickinson and Company`.
  - A line after a ";" is not joined to the one before (`:389–390`), so two chunks remain.
  - `readGrantNotice` then gives `{"acquiring":"Becton","acquired":"Dickinson and Company","entities":[],"acquiringEntities":["Accuri Cytometers, Inc."]}`. The company bought becomes the buyer's entity.
  - check:hsr reads this notice but asserts only JLL's grant.
- **The check pins the bug.** check:hsr's "…a comma before a legal word keeps a name whole ("Warburg, Pincus Capital Company, L.P." is two parties…)" asserts that `splitParties(...)` includes "Pincus Capital Company, L.P." (`scripts/check-hsr.ts:114`). README says the same (`README.md:1323`). That is one fund, not two parties.

**What users see:** NSC's and ITT's pages present the company as bought by "Clayton". PEP's and THC's pages carry rows of fragment names. BDX's page lacks four acquisitions of its own. EDGAR's former names, which ingest reads and this replay does not, reach more pages.

**What a fix would need:**
- Read a comma-joined run of 1994–1998 by its fixed order and count of parties, not at every comma; or keep a cell whole where splitting leaves a one-word party.
- Join a 2011 line that follows a ";".
- Re-pin check:hsr against the fund names and 20110575.

*Importance:* high: wrong statements of who bought whom on large pages · *Efficiency:* one splitter and one join rule · *Practicality:* moderate.

### 2. Impossible days in the committed merger file: `1998-01-00` makes Postgres refuse Merck's whole answer on every run, and two 1997 grants are dated 2007 · `P2`

**How sure:** high. The store failure was reproduced on PostgreSQL 16 with `upsertEvent`.

**Evidence**
- **Eight grants of notice 98-2575 (published 1998-02-03) have the day `1998-01-00`** (98-0772 to 98-1075). One is 98-0918, "Merck & Co., Inc.", the acquiring person.
- **`hsrEvents` keeps it.** It compares strings (`if (g.day < from) continue;`, `lib/hsrEarly.ts:541`), so the event's date is "1998-01-00".
- **Harness on PostgreSQL 16:**
  - `hsrFor` for MRK gives 37 events, one dated 1998-01-00.
  - They are stored in one transaction, as `runSource` stores them (`scripts/ingest.ts:288–320`). The result is `ROLLBACK: error: date/time field value out of range: "1998-01-00"`, and 0 `hsr_early` rows on MRK.
  - `runSource` logs an error and stores nothing, so the same thing happens on every run.
- **Two days fall after their own notice:**
  - 97-2402 is dated 2007-06-16 in notice 97-17035 of 1997-06-30.
  - 98-0612 is dated 2007-12-11 in notice 98-1365 of 1998-01-21: Central Garden & Pet Company's purchase of T.F.H. Publications.
  - `hsrEvents` puts the second on CIK 887733's pages (CENT and CENTA) on 2007-12-11.
- **Nothing checks days.** check:hsr asserts no day is a calendar date or on or before its notice's publication. Its only day assertions are the `!g.day` count and `number@day` uniqueness (`scripts/check-hsr.ts:169`).

**What users see:**
- MRK's page holds none of its 37 merger rows, and its run logs an error every refresh.
- CENT's and CENTA's charts show a 1997 deal ten years late.

**What a fix would need:** treat a day that is not a calendar date, or that falls after its notice's publication, as dayless (no row) in `sync:hsr` or `hsrGrants`, and assert it in check:hsr.

*Importance:* medium to high: one large page loses the whole source, and one row is ten years off · *Efficiency:* a few lines · *Practicality:* easy.

- **63 transaction numbers are in two notices, every one on two different days.** check:hsr's "…a transaction two notices print once" asserts only that `number@day` is unique (`scripts/check-hsr.ts:169`).
- **Pages that get one grant twice** (index names, from first prices):
  - **XOM:** 2010-12-23 and 2010-12-27 (20110398, notices 2011-334 and 2011-3174).
  - **ASH:** 2010-12-23 and 2010-12-27 (20110396).
  - **JNJ:** 1999-01-04 and 1999-01-05 (19991066, 99-2489 and 99-6121).
  - **XRX:** 1995-02-27 and 1996-01-15 (95-1065, Exor's purchase of Constitution Re from Xerox, 95-6752 and 96-3264).
- **24 of the 63 are two different deals.** For example:
  - 19984717 is both Morgan Stanley Dean Witter & Co.'s purchase of Toy Biz (1998-09-25) and Cogeneration Corporation of America's purchase from Northern States Power (1998-09-29).
  - 20040837 is both MacDonald Dettwiler's purchase of Marshall & Swift and Itron's purchase from Schlumberger.

**What users see:** the same deal twice on a chart, one of the two days wrong, eleven months apart on XRX. There is also a latent "Also on" between unrelated deals.

**What a fix would need:**
- Keep one grant a number where the parties agree (the first notice's, as the comment says).
- Where the parties differ, give the row's id the day too (`number@day`).
- Make check:hsr assert what its name says.

*Importance:* medium · *Efficiency:* a dedupe rule and an id change · *Practicality:* easy.

### 4. FDA documents dated by a sentence in their text or by a revision note, not the release line: Lilly's 1989 recall paper is 24 days early, Walmart's and Kroger's 2007 pet-food recall 4 days late · `P2`

**How sure:** high for the three company days. The committed file keeps only each document's lead and sentences, so the scan can miss others.

**Evidence**
- **How the day is read.** `headDate` takes the first spelled day of the document's year in its first 1,500 characters, before any day in figures (`lib/fdaHistory.ts:65–82`).
  - An OCR'd head day such as "Sept. l8, 1989" fails `\d{1,2}`, so a later day in the text wins.
  - A revision note printed above the release line wins over that line.
- **LLY, document 5b46d9daeba961a7.**
  - The row is dated 1989-08-25, and its own title reads "FDA talk paper: T89-55 Donald C McLearn Sept. l8, 1989 - Lilly Recalls Drugs Following FDA Inspections…".
  - Its lead begins mid-sentence: "found a number of conditions that were not in compliance…".
  - Lilly's later paper 6b08a62b95ee5d39 says "(Updates Talk Paper T89-55. Sept. 18. 1989)".
  - The row is on LLY's page: first price 1972-06-01, and no approval that day.
- **WMT and KR, document f78a459d1ea537b8.**
  - The rows are dated 2007-03-21.
  - The lead: "to include changes in the second paragraph. It was revised again on March 24, 2007 … FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE March 17, 2007 Recall of Pet Foods Manufactured by Menu Foods, Inc.".
- **What the check covers.** check:fda-history's "…none where … the OCR spoils it" uses a document whose only day is spoiled (`scripts/check-fda-history.ts:101`). Nothing tests a spoiled head with a good day after it.
  - 18 of the 530 kept leads begin in lower case.

**What users see:** the recall on LLY's chart three weeks before its paper, and the Menu Foods recall after the market had moved.

**What a fix would need:**
- Read the day beside the release line or the talk paper's number first.
- Treat a day followed by lower-case prose as a body day, and a "revised on" day as no head.
- Add one check of each kind.

*Importance:* medium: wrong days are the product's core harm, on few rows · *Efficiency:* one rule · *Practicality:* moderate.

### 5. A same-day release is joined to an approval by day alone: Strattera's row carries Forteo's talk paper, Primaxin's a Wilson's disease release, and the right release is on no row · `P2`

**How sure:** high. These are `approvalEvents` with `releasesByDay` and `fdaHistoryEvents` with `approvalDays`, as the two ingest steps call them.

**Evidence**
- **How the join works.**
  - `releasesByDay` keeps one document a day: the first in file order (day, then id) (`lib/fdaHistory.ts:382–386`).
  - `approvalEvents` adds it to every approval of that day (`lib/fdaApprovals.ts:295`).
  - `fdaHistoryEvents` drops every document on an approval day (`lib/fdaHistory.ts:354`).
- **LLY 2002-11-26.** The page's one approval that day is Strattera (NDA 21411). Two talk papers are of that day:
  - d5b7bdc71b7c480e: "FDA APPROVES TERIPARATIDE…".
  - e66edbcb6cfb7945: "FDA Approves Non-Stimulant ADHD Drug … Strattera".
  - The Strattera row reads: The FDA's talk paper that day: "Teriparatide is manufactured by Eli Lilly and Company of Indianapolis, lnd., and will be marketed under the trade name Forteo." The Strattera paper is on no row.
- **MRK 1985-11-26.** The Primaxin row (imipenem, NDA 50587) carries: "In this case, Merck Sharp and Dohme at the urging of the FDA, the Foundation for the Study of Wilson's Disease…". That release is the trientine release, in two OCR copies (8dd216bafdf8c4c4, b0bf9440771cfb1a).
- **The other 37.** Of the 39 joined rows (the ledger's count, reproduced), these two are the only days with two documents. The other 37 quote a sentence about the approved drug or its maker.
- **What the check covers.** check:fda-history tests the join only on Epogen's day, which has one document.

**What a fix would need:** join a release only where it names the approval's trade name or ingredient, and keep any other document of the day as its own row.

*Importance:* medium: the FDA's words about one drug on another drug's row, on two large pages · *Efficiency:* one match rule · *Practicality:* easy.

### 6. ITC address fragments read as parties: 25 rows on pages list "of Korea", "No", "516006 TTE Corporation" and the like among the respondents · `P3`

**How sure:** high.

**Evidence**
- **The cause.** `ENDS_ENTRY` ends an entry at a line ending in "Republic", "Kingdom" or "Province" (`lib/itc337.ts:157–161`). The rest of the address then becomes an entry of its own, and `partyName` keeps it.
- **The committed file.** 126 of its 1,122 cases list a party under four characters, or one beginning "of", "and", "Inc." or a digit. Some digit-led names are real ("232 Technologies Inc.").
  - Examples: "of Korea" (5), "of Korea 100-052", "No" (2), "and/or" (2), "338 Nanjing Road (West)", "E. Pratt Street", "Cypress Semiconductor Corp.,3901 North First Street,San Jose".
- **On pages, from first prices:** 25 institution rows list one. For example:
  - AAPL 2025-03-26, 337-TA-1443: "…Motorola (Wuhan) Mobility Technologies; 178 Xinfe…".
  - AMD 2022-06-07, 337-TA-1318: "…516006 TTE Corporation…".

**What a fix would need:** join a country word's next line to its entry, and drop an entry that starts with a lower-case word or is only a number or an address.

*Importance:* low: noise in a party list, no wrong match found · *Efficiency:* small · *Practicality:* easy.
