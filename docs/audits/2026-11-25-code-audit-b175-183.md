## Findings

### 1. Insider rows publish a filer's price slip as stated: AMD's page says Mubadala sold 40,000,000 shares for "about $21024000 billion", and 54 days pass the $1 million floor only by an impossible price · `P1`

**How sure:** high. These are the committed files rendered by the repository's own `insiderEvents` (harness `ins-amd.ts`, `ins-price.ts`).

**Evidence**
- **No guard on the price.** `stage1` turns a trade's value into `Math.round(shares * price)` for any `price > 0` (`scripts/sync-insiders.ts:111–113`). `insiderEvents` prints that value and value ÷ shares as "at about $X a share" (`lib/insiderTrades.ts:254`, `:267`).
- **The siblings have one.** The 13F and N-PORT readers both set a slipped price aside (`markSuspect`, `lib/instHoldings.ts:175`, `lib/fundHoldings.ts:93`). The insider reader has nothing like it.
- **AMD 2017-08-04, title as rendered:** "Mubadala Investment Co PJSC (director, 10% owner) sold 40,000,000 ADVANCED MICRO DEVICES INC shares, about $21024000 billion". The text says "at about $525600000.00 a share". $525,600,000 is 40,000,000 × $13.14, so the filing's price field evidently holds the sale's total.
- **Others:**
  - AGO 2008-04-08: "2 bought 21,303,792 shares (about $5325948 billion)", at $250,000,000 a share.
  - KYN 2025-10-17: MetLife "bought 25,000,000 … shares, about $325000 billion".
  - REEMF, ASTI, FINS, BTSGU, MGM 2016-09-01 (Tracinda, $100,000,000 a share), INVA.
- **Scale.**
  - 73 entries imply more than $1,000,000 a share, above any US stock's price. They fall on 63 days on 33 pages.
  - 54 of those 63 days would fall under `MIN_DAY_VALUE` without the impossible entry. They exist only because of the slip.
  - 137 days come to $10 billion or more.
- **Possibly a second cause.** Stage 1 reads no `SECURITY_TITLE` (`scripts/sync-insiders.ts:105–115`). A preferred share or a note reported in Table I is kept as "shares" of the company. MetLife's and Prudential's 25,000,000-unit purchases on closed-end funds (KYN, NHS, DNP, TYG) look like that, but the title cannot be checked offline.
- **The check tests nothing here.** check:insiders has no price-plausibility case (`scripts/check-insiders.ts:107–122`).

**Failure scenario:** a reader of AMD's page sees a $21 quadrillion insider sale in August 2017. The same rows count toward "· N sources" and the day's scoring.

**What a fix would need:** set aside a trade whose price is implausible, against the same day's other trades or a hard ceiling, as the 13F reader's `markSuspect` does, and keep only common-stock titles. Then re-sync.

*Importance:* high: absurd dollar figures on large-company pages · *Efficiency:* small · *Practicality:* easy; the cache holds the prices.

### 2. One block reported by several related filers is added once per filer: Antero's $744 million sale of 2014-05-21 is "13 sold 156,000,000 shares (about $9.7 billion)" · `P1`

**How sure:** high. These are committed rows rendered by `insiderEvents` (harness `ins-row.ts`, `ins-dupe2.ts`).

**Evidence**
- **Joint filers are counted once only within one filing.** "A filing is its first reporting owner's, the joint filers counted, as they report the same shares" (`lib/insiderTrades.ts:44`; `scripts/sync-insiders.ts:94–104`).
- **Separate filings become separate entries.** When related owners file separately for the same block (a fund's general partner, its members, its directors), `companyDays` keys an entry by `ownerCik|code` (`lib/insiderTrades.ts:142`). The title then sums the entries' shares and values (`:259–261`), and `bigEnough` sums them too (`:124–127`).
- **AR 2014-05-21.** Thirteen entries each "S 12000000 744000000": Antero Resources Investment LLC, Warburg Pincus Private Equity X O&G (and 9 joint filers), WP Antero (and 6), RADY PAUL M, Warren Glen C Jr and eight more. Title: "Insiders' trades in ANTERO RESOURCES Corp: 13 sold 156,000,000 shares (about $9.7 billion), led by Antero Resources Investment LLC".
- **Scale.**
  - 5,056 committed days hold the same code, shares (10,000 or more), trade days and value under two or more owners, up to 16 copies (PAYC 2015-09-30, 16 of 18 entries).
  - 638 of those days reach $1 million only through the repeats.
  - AGO 2008-04-08 is one of them, doubled on top of finding I1.

**Failure scenario:** a page reads as if insiders dumped thirteen times the stock they did. A $77,000 block reported by 14 affiliated filers clears the $1 million floor.

**What a fix would need:** treat entries of one day with the same code, shares, trade days and value as one block, naming its filers. Count the block once in the title and in `dayValue`.

*Importance:* high: wrong totals in titles · *Efficiency:* small · *Practicality:* easy; it needs no new data.

### 3. N-PORT reads splits that did not happen: an offering's 1.5× stake growth in a falling price, and one fund's own 2× or 1.5× change against five funds' "no split" · `P2`

**How sure:** high. The committed files contradict each other; the second cause is reproduced with the repository's own `compareMonth`.

**Evidence**
- **Cause (a), the group's split.** `compareMonth` takes the median ratio of five or more funds that held through and accepts a simple ratio where the median price moved the other way within `priceAgrees`' tolerance, a fifth for 1.5 and 2 (`lib/fundHoldings.ts:276`, `:108–112`). An offering that grows index funds' stakes by about half while the price falls 20–45% passes. The 13F veto runs one way only: `quarterSplitsIn` keeps only 13F quarters whose split is not 1 (`lib/fundHoldings.ts:131–133`), so a 13F quarter read as no split from hundreds of managers never overrules the funds (`:306`).
- **The committed files disagree.** A replay over `data/nport/` and `data/13f/` (harness `np-conflict2.ts`): 45 stored fund-page entries on 27 security-windows carry a split while every overlapping 13F quarter reads split 1 with five or more managers compared.
  - **HE, 2024-07-31 → 2024-10-31, split 1.5.** The 13F quarter 2024-09-30 reads split 1 from 328 managers, Vanguard Group +5,649,935 to 17,119,819 (×1.49: the September 2024 offering).
  - First Trust Small Cap Value AlphaDEX Fund's row says "−176,750 to 0" for a position the replay implies was 117,833 shares, and "the report before read after a 1.5-for-1 split". First Trust Small Cap Core AlphaDEX is the same at −444,605.
  - **Others:** CNM 2021-12 → 2022-03 (4 entries), BLNK 2024-06 → 2024-09 (3), CRCL 2026-03 → 2026-06 (3), CAI, ABNB, TLRY, XP, VG (2-for-1), FIZZ (2-for-1, 3 entries).
  - Five of the 45 are closed positions. Their sale is inflated by the false factor.
- **What is lost.** Every held-through fund in those windows has its real purchase erased (`was = round(before × split)` equals `after`, `:314–316`). That count needs the cache.
- **Cause (b), one fund's own split.** Where five or more funds held through and show no split (`gs === 1`), the fund's own `heldSplit` is still applied (`:300`). Its docstring limits it to "where fewer than five funds held the security through the same two months" (`:118–122`).
- **Reproduced (harness `nport-split.ts`).** Six funds unchanged while the price halves, and a seventh that doubles from 10,000 to 20,000 shares of a 1,000,000-share company. The seventh's +10,000 (a 1% move) gives `changes: [], moves: []`. The same holds for a fund halving its stake as the price doubles, and one adding half as the price falls a third.
- **The check never isolates either case.** check:nport tests `heldSplit` only for fewer than five funds (`scripts/check-nport.ts:111–112`). Its offering case keeps the price unmoved (`:139–145`).

**Failure scenario:** HE's 2024 offering. Each small-cap index fund's purchase between July and October 2024 is gone from HE's page and from the funds' pages. Two First Trust funds show sales half again as large as what they held. A fund that doubled into the March 2020 crash shows no purchase.

**What a fix would need:** let a 13F quarter read as split 1 (with enough managers) veto the funds' split for the overlapping windows. Apply `heldSplit` only when the group is undetermined (`gs === undefined`), as its docstring says.

*Importance:* medium-high: wrong share counts on fund pages, and erased 1% moves on company pages · *Efficiency:* two conditions · *Practicality:* easy, then a re-sync.

### 4. 13F reads an all-stock merger as a three-for-two split: First Citizens' CIT quarter, Agnico Eagle's Kirkland Lake quarter and Mativ's Neenah quarter are each "read after a 1.5-for-1 split" · `P2`

**How sure:** high that the split is false for FCNCA, by the committed H.2 file. AEM and MATV are named from the same pattern.

**Evidence**
- **No price check in the 13F split reader.** `pageQuarter` takes `splitFactor(ratios)`, the median of the managers' share ratios alone (`lib/instHoldings.ts:137–141`, `:248`). Each `Holding` carries its stated `value` (`:159`), so the price was available.
- **N-PORT has the guard 13F lacks.** batch 180 gave N-PORT `priceAgrees` after a first run read Eledon's financing as a 14-for-1 split (README, N-PORT section). 13F never got it.
- **FCNCA 2022-03-31.** In `data/13f/2022.json.gz`: split 1.5, 390 managers compared, net "+2,139,239", Vanguard "+300,616 to 1,105,554", BlackRock "+297,128 to 1,423,199".
  - The committed `data/fed-h2.json.gz` holds the Board's approval, week of 2021-12-18: "First Citizens Bancshares, Inc., Raleigh, North Carolina — to acquire CIT Group, Inc." That merger, paid in FCNCA stock, closed in this quarter.
  - The page's text says the quarter before was "read after a 1.5-for-1 split", and every holder's change is computed on prior holdings × 1.5.
- **The same pattern** appears in AEM 2022-03-31 (split 1.5, 580 compared, the Kirkland Lake merger quarter) and MATV 2022-09-30 (split 1.5, 225 compared, the SWM–Neenah merger). MATV's quarter also publishes ten 1% moves measured on the false factor.
- **It spreads to N-PORT.** A 13F split that is not 1 blocks N-PORT's held-through comparisons for every overlapping window (`lib/fundHoldings.ts:306–312`).

**Failure scenario:** FCNCA's Q1 2022 summary names Vanguard as a buyer of 300,616 shares. Its real change was about 568,900, the CIT conversion. A holder that kept its FCNCA shares and never held CIT reads as selling a third.

**What a fix would need:** require the managers' median price to move the other way, as N-PORT's `priceAgrees` does, before reading a split. The values are already in the stage files.

*Importance:* medium: wrong holder changes on merger quarters of large companies · *Efficiency:* one condition · *Practicality:* easy, then a re-sync.

### 6. An entry with one unpriced trade loses its priced trades' value, so a $50 million sale can fall under the $1 million floor · `P3`

**How sure:** high for the mechanism, by harness `ins-value.ts`. The number of days lost needs the cache.

**Evidence**
- **The merge.** In `companyDays` the merge reads `e.value = e.value === undefined || t.value === undefined ? undefined : e.value + t.value` (`lib/insiderTrades.ts:147`). `dayValue` then adds `e.value ?? 0` (`:124`).
- **The doc says otherwise.** `MIN_DAY_VALUE`'s doc: "a trade the filing gives no price for adds nothing" (`:119`).
- **Reproduced.** One filing with a 1,000,000-share sale worth $50,000,000 and a 100-share sale with no price gives `dayValue 0, bigEnough false`, in either order.
- **Unpriced entries are real.** 141 entries in the committed days have no value. One is Pfizer's 99,015,000 ZTS shares of 2013-02-08, a day kept by other entries.

**What a fix would need:** keep a priced subtotal per entry for `dayValue`, separate from whether the entry can show a total.

*Importance:* low to medium · *Efficiency:* one field · *Practicality:* easy.

### 7. `shareBasis` keeps a count stated before a reverse split, so 1% moves in that quarter are measured against a base up to 20 times too large · `P3`

**How sure:** high for the mechanism (harness `basis.ts`). How often the frames hold a pre-reverse-split count needs the cache.

**Evidence**
- **The loop.** `shareBasis` tries `[stated, stated * factor]` and returns the first `x` with `held <= 1.5 * x` (`lib/instHoldings.ts:147–151`). For a reverse split (factor < 1) the pre-split count is the larger, so it always passes first.
- **The docs say otherwise.** The module doc says "a share count stated before the split is read in the later unit (`shareBasis`)" (`:22–23`). README's 13F section says the same.
- **Reproduced.** `shareBasis(200_000_000, 8_000_000, 0.05)` gives 200,000,000; the post-split count is 10,000,000.
  - In a full `pageQuarter` with six managers through a one-for-twenty split, one manager's sale of 400,000 of 10,000,000 shares (4%) comes out as `bp: 20` and no move.
  - The forward case is right: `shareBasis(1_000_000, 8_000_000, 10)` gives 10,000,000.

**What a fix would need:** for a reverse split, prefer `stated * factor` when the managers' holdings are far below `stated`, as the forward branch does with the 1.5× test.

*Importance:* low: missed rows, not wrong ones · *Efficiency:* one comparison · *Practicality:* easy.

### 8. `sync:supreme-court` drops a volume govinfo fails to serve and still writes the file, so up to 19 volumes' cases can vanish and ingest unlinks them · `P3`

**How sure:** high for the mechanism. It needs one bad govinfo answer per volume. This is the class the last audit's finding 4 raised for the EU mergers, which batch 175 fixed there and batches 177 and 183 copied, but not here.

**Evidence**
- **A failed volume is read as not held.** `get` returns null for a 404 and for any 3xx, because "a volume govinfo does not hold answers 302 to an error page" (`scripts/sync-supreme-court.ts:49`, `:58`). The volume loop then pushes the volume to `missing` and skips its cases (`:93–96`).
- **The only guard is a floor.** It throws only under 200 volumes (`:104`). The committed file holds 219 (368 to 586) with `missing: []`.
- **The held list is never compared.** `held.volumes` is loaded (`:86`) but not checked against this run's volumes. The batch 175 guard ("a year's list never shorter than the file's") is in `scripts/sync-eu-mergers.ts`, `sync-eu-antitrust.ts:102–103` and `sync-eu-courts.ts:94`, but not here.
- **What follows.** `supremeCourtFor` answers `complete: {}` (`lib/ingest/supremeCourtFor.ts:22`), and `runSource` then calls `unlinkGone` (`scripts/ingest.ts:320–321`).

**Failure scenario:** govinfo redirects a dozen volume records to its error page during one monthly run. The sync writes a file without those volumes' cases (Chevron v. NRDC's volume among them), and the next ingest takes those rows off every company page.

**What a fix would need:** fail when a volume the file holds is missing, as the EU syncs do with their year lists.

*Importance:* low to medium: silent loss of rows · *Efficiency:* two lines · *Practicality:* easy.

### 9. EU-court rows name other parties as the company ("Matina Stevi and The New York Times Company the applicant"), and their unit rows have no free relevance rule · `P3`

**What a fix would need (both parts):** split at " and " where the company's own part ends in a legal word; drop the trailing `\b` after a word ending in a non-ASCII letter, or add the `u` flag. Add an `eu_courts` branch beside `eu_antitrust`, with a check line.

## Set aside (9)

1. **Unmerged EU-court duplicates.** 99 same-day pairs of EU-court judgments share a case number but were not merged. `mergeJudgments` keys on `cases[0]|day`, and a notice's case list can lead with another number (1978-10-25: C-103/77, C-145/77 beside C-145/77). No listed page holds one: a replay of all 312 company days found none.
2. **EU fine figures with four or more decimals.** `FIGURE` reads at most three decimals before "million" (`lib/euAntitrust.ts:350`), so "EUR 24,7416 million" is stored as EUR 24 (Wieland Werke). ICI is stored as EUR 91 and Arkema as EUR 219 (methacrylates, 2006). None reaches a listed page (180-row replay).
   - HTHIY's 2010-05-19 row does show a junk name: "on Jointly and severally on Elpida Memory, Inc.".
3. **Insider trade days after the filing day.** 31 committed entries have a trade day after their filing day, as filed, with no guard. One is Munch Mark Robert's filing of 2010-02-19, whose trade reads 2020-02-19.
4. **Restatements move 13F and N-PORT rows.** A restatement moves a 13F or N-PORT row to the amendment's own filing day; insiders keep the original's day. 2,423 13F 1% moves are dated more than a year after their quarter, as are 28 N-PORT moves and 222 fund-page rows.
   - Example: BNY Mellon's Q2 2014 restatement, filed 2015-08-28, carries its "+92,670,971 AAPL" with that day.
   - This matches the documented "on its report's day", so it is a design choice. It is not consistent with the insider rule.
5. **13F unit-slip exemption.** `markSuspect` lets a price 1/1000 of the median pass as a value stated in the wrong unit (`lib/instHoldings.ts:183`). Shares stated ×1000 produce the same ratio.
   - 49 moves claim 50% or more of a company. Some are real (Bain on BURL 2014, Mithaq on PLCE 2024).
   - Two look like slips: BNP Paribas Financial Markets "−13,789,797" CACI shares (58.9%, 2013-09-30), and Nuveen "−5,500,527" CTRN (66.3%, 2025-06-30, a quarter whose net is −5,763,276).
   - This cannot be settled without the cache.
6. **Insider amendments that remove every P/S trade.** Stage 1 keeps only filings with a P or S trade (`scripts/sync-insiders.ts:116`). A 4/A that corrects a mistaken sale to another code never reaches `effectiveFilings`, and the original sale stands. This cannot be measured offline.
7. **Insider roles from joint filers.** `rel` is the OR of every reporting owner (`scripts/sync-insiders.ts:97`), so "Icarus Investment Corp. (Secretary, director, 10% owner)". 3,420 entity entries take a director or officer role from a joint filer. The row does say "and N joint filers".
8. **Honeywell Aerospace GmbH's switch day.** The unit moves from HON to HONA on 2025-10-01, the spin-off record's first filing, not the separation. No judgment falls between.
9. **RIO and Kennecott.** RIO matches the 1965 *Kennecott Copper Corp. v. United States* through EPA's undated Kennecott unit (`releaseNamesOn` gives "Kennecott" in 1965), years before Rio Tinto owned it. RIO's first price likely hides the row; the fixtures do not say.
